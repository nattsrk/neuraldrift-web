Built by people who have
stood the watch

Neural Drift wasn't started by a software company that discovered shipping. It was started in Singapore by seafarers who got tired of decisions made on stale spreadsheets — and built the tool they wished the office had.

The story

From the water up

We began as an ROV inspection outfit: real dives, real hulls, real fouling — and reports that clients filed away and forgot. The waste was obvious. The same inspection that answered "how bad is it?" could answer "what is it costing you, and when should you act?" — if anyone connected it to the vessel's own operating data. Nobody had. So we built GE6, the engine that does, and grew it into the platform: hull evidence, noon reports, weather, bunkers and compliance on one spine. In 2026 we unified everything under one name — Neural Drift.

Evidence first

We measure before we model. Our own ROV puts ground truth under every hull score — the thing pure-software platforms can't do.

A dash, never a guess

Missing data renders as "—" across the entire platform. No invented figures, no silent averages. It's a build rule, not a slogan.

Deterministic where it counts

Regulated numbers come from tested, deterministic engines. AI helps people read and write; it never authors a compliance figure.

Made for the sea

Field and bridge tools assume bad connectivity, gloves and time pressure — because we work in those conditions ourselves.
